During Ramadan, ABF launched **[bangaloresehri.in](https://bangaloresehri.in)** — a lifeline for students, single professionals, and especially women in PGs and hostels who struggle to access Sehri (the pre-dawn meal before the daily fast).

## How it started

In 2024, the first three weeks were enough to validate the need: **over 5 lakh views** and **4,000+ direct requests** for Sehri assistance. A live Google map with pinned providers made discovery seamless. The platform divided Bengaluru into North/South/East/West, with a one-tap toggle for "I'm looking for Sehri" vs "I'm providing Sehri."

## How it grew

By 2025 and 2026, the platform had deepened from utility to movement:

- **100+ Sehri providers** came on board (homes, restaurants, masjids, community kitchens).
- **50,000+ people** received Sehri daily during the holy month.
- **1.5 lakh individuals** gathered at masjids across Bangalore for community Sehri.

## Honouring the unsung heroes

In April 2025, ABF hosted a **Meet & Greet** for the Sehri providers who featured on the platform. The gathering was attended by prominent personalities of Karnataka. The energy in the hall confirmed what we'd suspected: this was no longer a directory. It was a community recognising itself.

## What this teaches us

A static map plus a phone number is enough to coordinate compassion at scale. The technology is trivial; the trust and the network behind it are what matter.
