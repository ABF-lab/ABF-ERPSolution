The drug epidemic in parts of Bengaluru is real, escalating, and devastating young lives. Raah-E-Nijat — *"the path to salvation"* — is ABF's community-driven response.

## Our strategy

We don't run a rehab clinic. We run a **network**.

- **Education** — youth workshops on the science and reality of addiction, plus skills to resist peer pressure.
- **Coalition-building** — convening parents, teachers, local leaders, former users, and law enforcement so that nobody is fighting the problem alone.
- **Safe spaces** — sports, arts, and skill-building programmes that crowd out the alternative.
- **Awareness campaigns** — reaching whole neighbourhoods through masjids, schools, and community halls.
- **Rehab referrals** — direct linkage to government de-addiction centres, NIMHANS, and partner mental-health services.

## Coordination with the state

In December 2023 ABF convened a **joint meeting with the Karnataka Minority Commission and senior police officials** at the Drugs Control Department. NGOs from across Bengaluru attended. Police officials committed to intensifying enforcement to disrupt drug supply chains; we committed to mobilising the community on the demand side.

The work continues — most recently through community awareness sessions held in February and March 2025, addressing both government-scheme literacy and substance abuse in the same conversation.

## Why this approach

Addiction is rarely solved by punishment alone. The community has to want a different future for its young people. Raah-E-Nijat is the scaffolding for that — equipping local teams to lead, and being the connective tissue between families, professionals, and the state.
